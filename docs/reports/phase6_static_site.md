# Phase 6 — Static site

**Status:** built and rendering the real Week 1 data; visual review pending (no browser in the
build session). **Date:** 2026-09-08.

## What was built

**`site_build.py`** — `python -m nfl_predict.site_build` renders `site/index.html` from
`data/predictions/`, `data/results/` and `data/backtest.json`. Nothing else: no JavaScript, no
fetches, no framework. Charts are inline SVG drawn in Python. Past weeks collapse with
`<details>`. Every number on the page links to the committed JSON file it came from.

`data/backtest.json` moved out of the gitignored `data/processed/` and is now committed: it is
the model's stated pre-live track record, regenerated by every retrain, and the site shows it.

## What the page shows

1. **This week's ledger** — one row per game, the official prediction (latest pass before that
   game's kickoff): kickoff in ET, matchup, our pick, our home-win probability, a **spread
   gauge** with our margin (circle) and the Vegas line (diamond) on one axis, the Vegas
   probability, and — once played — the winner with ✓/✗ for both us and Vegas, the score, and
   the ATS result. Under it, a provenance line per pass: published when, how many hours before
   the first kickoff, model trained through which game, which commit, with links to the
   prediction file and the odds snapshot.
2. **Season to date** (once results exist) — the summary strip, straight-up accuracy by week
   for model and Vegas, and a calibration chart once there are 30+ graded games.
3. **Earlier weeks**, collapsed.
4. **Before going live** — the 2021–2025 backtest table per season and its calibration chart,
   with the sentence "The model does not beat Vegas" and the exact gap, so the bar the live
   season is measured against is on the page itself.
5. **How to check any of this** — the gate, immutability, and links to the data directories
   and the scheduled jobs.

## Design

The subject is a verifiable record, so evidence sits with the data rather than in a footer,
and the hero is the ledger, not a headline number. One element carries the visual weight — the
per-game spread gauge — and everything else is quiet. Bricolage Grotesque for headings and
numbers (tabular figures), Source Sans 3 for body. Pale bone-grey ground, slate ink, model blue
and Vegas bronze; the two series colours were validated for colour-vision-deficiency separation
and contrast on both the light and dark surface with the dataviz validator, and the dark pair
was re-stepped once to pass the lightness band. Green/red are reserved for results and always
paired with a glyph. Dark mode follows the system setting. No all-caps labels, no decorative
motion.

## Verified

- Renders the real Week 1 file: 16 ledger rows, 16 gauges, all provenance links resolve to the
  committed paths. HTML parses with balanced tags. No `<script>`, no `http://`, no `apiKey`.
- 9 tests covering every data state: no predictions, an upcoming week, a graded week (winner
  and marks), a late pass superseding the early one, past weeks collapsing, the backtest
  section with a hollow small-n bin, gauge clamping and labels, and a real build.
- `%-d`/`%-I` in `strftime` are glibc-only and crash on Windows; dates are formatted by hand.
- `pytest` → 81 passed offline; `ruff` clean.

## Wired into automation

Each cron workflow now runs `site_build` after its data step and commits `site/` with the data,
so the page updates on every publish and every grade. The exit criterion — the page correctly
rendering a full round trip of predicted week, baseline and graded result — is met with test
fixtures; the real round trip completes when Week 1 is graded on Tuesday.
